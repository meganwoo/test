---
title: "What did this object mean to the Moche people?"
---

While this artifact is widely regarded as an artistic piece, the role it played in Moche culture was likely enough to elevate it to an item of religious and ritualistic significance. The storage of maize beer held specific importance as a common ritual for the recently departed. In addition, the iconography composed of fine line paintings on the body of the vessel were often religious, naturalistic, or sexual in nature. This vessel in particular may exist to highlight the connection of natural figures, such as lizards, to Moche religious practices. 

The stirrup spout design holds religious meaning as well. This particular shape was only used in ceramics holding a dedicated, sacred purpose, typically reserved for the pouring and storage of Chicha. The shape also preserved potential hallucinogenic and, by extension, visionary properties of the contained liquid, allowing priests to safely ingest liquids with minimal atmospheric interaction.
