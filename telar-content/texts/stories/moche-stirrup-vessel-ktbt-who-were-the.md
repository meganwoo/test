title: "Who were the people that made this vessel?"
---

The Moche were a civilization based in the northern coast of Peru around 100-800 CE. Rather than being one centralized civilization, they consisted of many small regional civilizations that connected in religion, art, and culture. Together, they formed a hierarchical and stratified kingdom with rulers, priests, artisans, and farmers. Due to their dry desert environment, they learned to adapt to the arid conditions with intricate irrigation systems and farming. These complex agricultural technologies allowed them to succeed despite the harsher conditions. Additionally, religion was a very important factor in Moche culture. With many sacrificial rituals, artistic symbolism, and ceremonies, the Moche people's religion was an integral part in their daily lives. Religion also had strong ties to those in power, who often used religion to give them rights to authority. The prevalence of their religion is seen in religious architecture, most notably the Huaca del Sol, as well as Moche art. Ceramics, sculptures, and paintings depicted religious figures and created vessels and artwork commonly used in ceremonies and rituals.

One key example of Moche art is their ceramics. There was a great amount of Moche ceramics, most of which came in 9 basic shapes, like the stirrup-spout bottle. Many Moche ceramics were intricately created and decorated with paintings of animalistic figures and humans. Some of these anthropomorphic figures depicted were camelids, monkeys, rodents, birds, and many others. Many of these paintings depicted a story or scene involving the figures, often with a religious and cultural context behind it. While these vessels were initially thought to mostly be seen in burials, they are also hypothesized to serve in more everyday, household contexts as well.

The importance of Moche art had a significant impact on their culture. Religion and art were often interwoven, as seen in many of their artworks depicting religious deities. This created a large amount of intricate and meaningful objects that are now famous and closely analyzed. Additionally, it also impacted other civilizations as well. While it was an integral part of their society back then, it also had great effects on following civilizations like the Chimu, who were inspired by such traditions and artstyle.
