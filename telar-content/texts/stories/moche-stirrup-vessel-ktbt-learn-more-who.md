---
title: "Learn more: Who are the Moche, and what was their culture?"
---

The Moche were a civilization based in the northern coast of Peru around 100-800 CE. Rather than being one centralized civilization, they consisted of many small regional civilizations that connected in religion, art, and culture. With many sacrificial rituals, artistic symbolism, and ceremonies, the Moche people's religion was an integral part in their daily lives. The prevalence of their religion is seen in depictions of religious figures in their artwork.

One key example of Moche art is their ceramics. There was a great amount of Moche ceramics, most of which came in 9 basic shapes, like the stirrup-spout bottle. Many Moche ceramics were intricately created and decorated with paintings of animalistic figures and humans. Some of these anthropomorphic figures depicted were camelids, monkeys, rodents, birds, and many others. Many of these paintings depicted a story or scene involving the figures, often with a religious and cultural context behind it. While these ceramic vessels were initially thought to mostly be seen in burials, they are also hypothesized to serve in more everyday, household contexts as well.

Moche culture had a significant impact on the art they created. Religion and art were often interwoven, as seen in the Moche stirrup spout vessel. This created a large amount of intricate and meaningful objects that are now famous and closely analyzed.
